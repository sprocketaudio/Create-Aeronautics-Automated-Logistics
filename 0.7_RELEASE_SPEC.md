# 0.7 Release Specification

## Status and authority

This is the active release scope for `0.7`. It collects the player-facing
compatibility, visual, map, cargo, and information features agreed for this
update, together with a record of the internal Drive Mode foundations built
during development.

**Drive Mode / the Advanced Transponder is not player-facing in this release.**
The Advanced Transponder and Drive Module remain unavailable as obtainable
blocks/items in `0.7`. Their implementation is retained internally for later
development; normal recorded-route playback remains the supported public mode.
See `SABLE_DRIVE_MODE.md` for current status, test findings, and the resumed
development plan.

Forge support is explicitly out of scope. The mod remains NeoForge-only.

## Release features

### 1. Internal Advanced Transponder / Drive Mode foundations

Retain the substantial prototype described in `SABLE_DRIVE_MODE.md` without
advertising or exposing it as a completed `0.7` feature. The following records
the implementation already present and the behavior that must be preserved
when development resumes; it is not a public `0.7` contract.

- The Advanced Transponder is the controller/brain for route-following intent.
- Linked Drive Modules are the player-configured, front-face redstone output
  endpoints placed where the ship's machinery actually lives.
- Modules must support safe persistence, same-vehicle linking, configuration,
  validation, unlinking, permissions, and clear player-facing health/error
  states.
- The first output contracts cover lift, forward/back, left/right, turn, and
  docking through the supported target-strength or binary roles.
- Lift-module roles explicitly select one of two independent flight-control
  paths. `Burner Strength` selects the airship controller and retains its
  analog burner calibration and predictive height control. A paired
  `Rotor Faster` and `Rotor Slower` selects the rotorcraft controller, which
  calibrates and controls a persistent rotor speed through timed pulses.
  Mixing burner and rotor lift roles, or configuring only one rotor role, is
  invalid. Airship guidance and saved calibration must never be altered by or
  passed through rotorcraft steering and lift logic.
- Route guidance calculates intent only. Player-built machinery remains solely
  responsible for real propulsion, lift, and steering; Drive Mode must not
  directly move, teleport, or materialize a ship along a route.
- Ordinary recorded samples use a bounded look-ahead instead of requiring the
  physical ship to enter every sample's exact 3D arrival radius. Guidance is
  paced by the physical ship: it advances when the ship reaches or passes the
  current sample and freezes when the ship falls beyond the allowed look-ahead,
  so the route clock cannot run away on its own. Initial guidance blends out
  from the ship's actual start pose so an off-course start or restart recovers
  toward the route without first flying backwards to an obsolete sample. Real
  stops and route endpoints still require physical arrival. In Drive Mode,
  recorded rotation is required only when the ship has a linked Turn Drive
  Module; ships without turn control use position-only arrival for ordinary
  stops and endpoints. A docking-style stop always requires alignment, and a
  Drive Mode route containing one must refuse to start unless turn control is
  linked and configured.
- Analog lift control treats its current burner strength as the ship's learned
  equilibrium. The Advanced Transponder provides a lift-calibration action
  which tests the assembled ship's linked lift output, proves both a rising and
  a falling strength, and learns neutral lift, burner response delay, measured
  ascent speed, and measured descent speed. After testing it must return to its
  starting height, switch off the test output, and settle before reporting
  success or saving the profile. A fractional neutral point is represented
  by blending only the two adjacent analog strengths. Drive Mode starts from
  the saved profile and slowly adapts it during stable flight so cargo and other
  weight changes do not require a full recalibration. It holds constant heat, observes the resulting altitude and
  vertical speed, and forecasts the gentle vertical speed needed to converge on
  the moving route height. It changes by only one redstone level, then holds
  that level through a burner-response observation window before making another
  judgment. The forecast includes acceleration in both directions: if an earlier
  adjustment is already slowing a climb or slowing a fall enough to approach the
  desired motion, the controller holds power instead of stacking another change.
  A repeated correction must also wait past the calibrated response boundary when
  the previous change has produced no observable acceleration. On a final approach,
  normal predictive braking remains in closed-loop control through arrival rather
  than replacing the working trim with an unvalidated fractional neutral shortcut.
  While holding a stationary
  target at neutral power, persistent upward or downward drift slowly corrects the
  saved neutral value using the calibrated ascent/descent response; moving route
  targets and deliberate non-neutral power must not contaminate that learning.
  Long descents may target up to 1.2 blocks/second relative to the route while
  ascent correction remains capped at 1.0 block/second; predictive braking still
  reduces descent speed before arrival.
  Active lift never commands zero, and being above or below the route does not
  itself demand less or more power when the ship's changing motion is already
  correcting it. Seamless schedule-leg and return-leg handoffs preserve the
  current lift trim and measured motion instead of resetting to neutral. A
  genuinely fresh controller starts from the assembled ship's measured vertical
  velocity when the vehicle API exposes it.
- Drive playback starts only for a resolved loaded assembled vehicle and must
  clear outputs and pause safely on stop, fault, module/controller loss, or
  vehicle-retention loss.
- Normal recorded playback remains the supported public movement mode.

### 2. Airship Station Display Link support

Add Create Display Link data-source support to the **Airship Station** only.

- A Display Link is attached directly to an Airship Station, following Create's
  normal source-block setup.
- Do not add Display Link sources to Ship Transponders or Logistics Terminals.
  Their shapes and normal use do not make a good visual attachment point.
- The Station source should support a station-board use case comparable to a
  Create train-station board.
- The initial information set should cover:
  - station name;
  - selected, assigned, approaching, or docked vehicle name where available;
  - station/route runtime state;
  - dock state and queue state;
  - cargo-transfer or cargo-wait state;
  - a concise blocked/failure reason when one exists.
- Output should be concise enough for flap displays, signs, Nixie tubes, and
  Display Boards. Do not promise unreliable ETA calculations.

### 3. Map integration controls and Xaero's World Map

- Add Xaero's World Map support for live automated vehicle markers.
- Match the established FTB Chunks/JourneyMap behaviour where the Xaero API
  allows it: vehicle name, transport icon, current position, and display state.
- Add separate client configuration switches for FTB Chunks, JourneyMap, and
  Xaero's World Map. Each defaults to enabled and removes or hides only this
  mod's markers cleanly when disabled.
- Keep map integrations optional and client-side. No map mod becomes a required
  dependency.

### 4. Cargo-link compatibility expansion

Extend the existing cargo-link model, without changing its ownership, preview,
or wait-condition semantics, to cover:

- Create: Connected Item Silos, including their vertical 1x1, 2x2, and 3x3
  storage layouts;
- Platinum Tanks from Propulsion: Simulated;
- IronVaults storage variants.

For each integration:

- detect the real exposed item/fluid capability rather than assuming a block is
  storage from its name;
- resolve its actual controller/root when the mod exposes one, so a multiblock
  is not double-counted;
- preserve the current moving-ship and station-side validity rules;
- reject an unsupported or invalid endpoint clearly rather than silently
  linking it.

### 5. Mechanical Bearing playback animation

During recorded automated playback, animate **Create Mechanical Bearings** on
the moving vehicle. This is the bearing used to mount sails, rotors, and other
Create rotating assemblies; it is not the Create Aeronautics propeller-bearing
block.

First-pass behaviour:

- animate only while the automated vehicle is actively moving;
- stop during normal waits, docking holds, fault holds, and stopped playback;
- remain client-side visual behaviour only: do not inject kinetic force, create
  a second contraption, or alter server-side bearing state;
- a fixed visual rotation speed/direction is acceptable for this release.

Recording and reproducing the original bearing's exact running state, speed,
and direction is future polish, not a `0.7` requirement.

## Release gates

- Test every optional compatibility integration with the relevant mod present
  and absent.
- Test a Station-attached Display Link with at least a flap display and a
  Display Board.
- Test Mechanical Bearing animation on a real automated airship carrying a
  sail/rotor assembly, including movement, wait, docking, stop, and fault
  transitions.
- Test JourneyMap disabled/enabled behaviour and Xaero marker refreshes without
  affecting FTB Chunks or the in-mod Logistics Terminal.
- Ensure existing routes, cargo links, maps, stations, and transponders remain
  compatible with prior releases.

## Explicitly deferred

- Forge port;
- public Advanced Transponder / Drive Module availability and the first public
  Drive Mode release;
- Drive Mode release-gate testing across representative airship steering,
  docking, reload, mass/cargo changes, and rotorcraft configurations;
- exact recorded Mechanical Bearing speed/direction reproduction;
- estimated arrival times on Display Link boards.
